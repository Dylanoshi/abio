# ABIO

**Exploring the emergence of life from computation.**

[abio.life](https://abio.life)

ABIO is an independent research initiative investigating computational abiogenesis, artificial life, and the conditions under which self-replicating, evolving systems can emerge spontaneously.

Our ambition is to understand how life-like organization can arise from simple computational rules—and ultimately whether we can create environments in which new forms of autonomous virtual life emerge without being explicitly designed.

## Our approach

We begin by reproducing and extending experiments in computational artificial life, documenting our methods, publishing observations, and investigating questions that emerge from the results.

Our research focuses on three related phenomena:

- **Emergence:** How do replication-capable configurations arise?
- **Establishment:** What allows a replicating lineage to persist across generations?
- **Evolution:** Under what conditions can persistent lineages develop heritable variation, adaptation, and increasing complexity?

Our guiding principle:

**We build the world. We don't build the organism.**

## Research publications

### Research Note 001 — Divergent Replication Trajectories in a Computational Chemistry

*October 26, 2026 · Preliminary research*

Our first research note examines two experimental trajectories using the BFF computational chemistry developed by Blaise Agüera y Arcas and collaborators.

One trajectory exhibited rapid proliferation of replication-positive programs. Another exhibited a transient replication-positive episode without sustained proliferation at sampled checkpoints.

These findings motivate further investigation into the distinction between replication emergence and successful lineage establishment. They do not yet establish the mechanisms responsible for these differences.

## Long-term vision

We are interested in whether computational environments can support the spontaneous emergence of autonomous, evolving virtual organisms.

If such systems can be established, we hope to investigate the conditions under which they develop increasingly complex organization, behavior, and potentially novel forms of information processing.

These are open scientific questions, not established outcomes.

## Open research

ABIO aims to make computational artificial-life research more accessible through public experiments, clear explanations, and transparent reporting.

We welcome curiosity, critical discussion, and independent replication.
