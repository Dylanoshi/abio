# ABIO E014 Research Draft (number pending)

## Selective Catalytic Inhibition in a Transient Computational Organization

**ABIO — Computational Abiogenesis Research**  
**Version:** 0.1 (public-review draft)  
**Prepared:** November 11, 2026  
**Release:** not scheduled; further experimentation prioritized  
**Status:** Exploratory, not peer reviewed; single retrospectively selected computational trajectory

### Abstract

We report a matched counterfactual analysis of a transient functional organization identified in ABIO's spatial particle-and-catalysis simulation (E011–E014). In an E013 screen of six parameter regimes with five seeds each, one retrospectively selected sham trajectory (regime 05, seed 3) satisfied a strict candidate heuristic for six consecutive steps (530–535). The criterion combined founder-material replacement, preserved catalytic motifs, recent motif-associated production, and regenerative-incorporation indicators.

Reconstructing the historical unperturbed trajectory reproduced the six-step episode. Inhibiting the selected functional lineage's catalytic activity from step 513 suppressed four opportunities and eliminated its strict-candidate observations, whereas a retrospectively designed **active off-target perturbation** suppressed three reactions and retained all six candidate steps. At step 535, target-blocked functional similarity was 0.2357 versus 0.7845 under sham and off-target suppression. Both the sham and off-target branches preserved the candidate's original motifs and regenerative-incorporation indicator; target blocking did not. However, all three tracked functional lineages disappeared from the detector at step 543. Thus the data support an exploratory, mechanism-sensitive effect on **functional organization**, not prolonged existence, formal RAF closure, or verified autonomous self-maintenance.

### 1. Research question

Can catalytic production by a spontaneously identified computational organization contribute causally to preservation of its functional configuration during material turnover? And is this distinguishable from nonspecific effects of interfering with unrelated catalytic reactions?

This note investigates a *retrospectively selected case*, not the population-level probability of emergence or a preregistered confirmatory hypothesis.

### 2. Substrate, screen, and operational definitions

The underlying E011 substrate comprises a spatial population of discrete particles, bonds, local catalytic interactions, supplied resources and turnover. Functional organizations are identified by simulation-specific lineage and catalytic-profile heuristics, **not by a proven boundary or a formal RAF algorithm**.

The source screen, E013, covered **six parameter regimes × five seeds = 30 regime–seed combinations**. The present study focuses on one observed case, **regime 05 / seed 3**, and must not treat six consecutive positive simulation steps as six independent observations.

The selected branch had the original motif labels `AA>A` and `AC>C`. These are internal substrate reaction signatures, not evidence of chemically realistic reaction stoichiometry or a food-generated, reflexively autocatalytic set.

The candidate's *strict* indicator is a conjunction of: (i) complete replacement of the tracked founding particles, (ii) exact preservation of original branch motifs, (iii) recent motif-linked catalytic production and (iv) a recent regenerative-incorporation indicator. This metric is deliberately stronger than detecting bonds or persistence, but it is **not a validated test of life**. In particular, disappearance from the tracked functional lineage can reflect a change in the identification heuristic rather than total material extinction.

### 3. Reconstruction and counterfactual protocol

The original E011 branch point was **step 360**. A first attempt to perturb only steps 530–535 reproduced the six sham positives but had **zero intervention exposure**: no relevant reaction was suppressed. This no-op result was not interpreted causally.

An event audit identified target-associated activity earlier in the history; intervention was moved to **step 513**. The original E011 trajectory was reconstructed from its recorded settings and seed, advanced without perturbation through step 512, and deep-copied into identical physical initial states for matched branches.

The main comparisons were:

- **Sham:** no catalytic inhibition.
- **Target-blocked:** inhibit production attributed to the selected functional lineage.
- **Off-target blocked:** inhibit scheduled catalytic opportunities attributed to other functional lineages. The schedule was designed **after examining this case**; it is exploratory, not a prospective randomized control.
- **Product-scrambled:** in a separate earlier analysis, change products of target-linked catalysis without simply preventing reaction opportunities.

Intervention exposure was evaluated explicitly. The nominal original `control_block` condition suppressed zero reactions in the key window and is **not counted as an active control**.

### 4. Results: selective disruption through step 535

| Outcome at step 535 unless indicated | Sham | Target blocked | Active off-target blocked |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Actually suppressed reactions, steps 513–535 | 0 | 4 | 3 |
| Strict-candidate positive steps, 530–535 | 6 | 0 | 6 |
| Functional similarity to selected profile | 0.7845 | 0.2357 | 0.7845 |
| Tracked functional size | 10 | 8 | 10 |
| Founding material completely replaced | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Original catalytic motifs preserved | Yes | No | Yes |
| Recent regenerative-incorporation indicator | 1 | 0 | 1 |

The physical trajectories of both inhibited branches first diverged from the matched sham at **step 513**. The originally planned off-target control schedule contained four events; only **three of four** occurred after the altered trajectory evolved. Therefore this experiment is **not event-count matched**. Even matching counts and steps would not match spatial location, catalytic centrality, product identity, or downstream impact.

A separate product-scramble condition exhibited six strict-candidate steps and a functional similarity of **0.6708** at step 535. This shows that physical product alteration need not eliminate the heuristic candidate in this specific case, but does not prove robustness to arbitrary product substitutions.

**Interpretation:** inhibiting the selected lineage changed preserved motifs, apparent regenerative incorporation and functional similarity, while active inhibition elsewhere did not produce the same measured difference. The strict-candidate signal is partly defined *by* motif and incorporation measurements; its disappearance cannot serve as an independent proof of a new causal phenomenon. The underlying functional-profile differences are more informative.

### 5. Results: extended observation through step 600

To examine persistence, the same reconstructed branches were continued from step 513 to step 600, without selecting a new target.

| Tracking result | Sham | Target blocked | Active off-target blocked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Steps with tracked functional size > 0 (513 onward) | 30 | 30 | 30 |
| First tracked absence | 543 | 543 | 543 |
| Reappearance after step 543 (through 600) | No | No | No |
| Exact motif preservation interval | 525–535 | None | 525–535 |
| Strict-candidate interval | 530–535 | None | 530–535 |

All three branches lost the **tracked functional identity** at step 543, with no subsequent reappearance reported through step 600. These observations do not prove that all associated physical particles vanished; the lineage identifier could cease to match a reorganized descendant. They show that target catalysis affected an **observed functional state** without changing its **observed lifespan** in this case.

### 6. Interpretation

The result is consistent with three distinguishable phenomena:

1. **Material/structural persistence:** a lineage remains trackable for a finite period.
2. **Functional preservation:** selected catalytic motifs and regenerative-incorporation indicators are retained within that period.
3. **Establishment:** continued organizational survival or reproduction across longer intervals and challenges.

The present case supplies exploratory evidence relevant to (2), but does not establish organizational autonomy or (3). Its natural tracked lifetime appears constrained by factors other than the inhibited catalytic process—or by the tracking method itself.

This motivates testing whether spatial organization, resource retention, bonding, lineage splitting, or detector-specific identity changes explain the common disappearance at step 543.

### 7. Limitations and claims explicitly **not** made

- **One retrospectively selected trajectory:** the six positive time steps are serially dependent, not independent replicates.
- **Post-selection bias:** the intervention start and off-target control schedule were informed by prior observation of the same seed.
- **Incomplete active-control matching:** 3 off-target suppressions versus 4 target suppressions; no matching for position or causal importance.
- **Tracking ambiguity:** the heuristic functional lineage may terminate while related components or organizations remain.
- **Operational circularity:** strict candidacy includes some of the metrics affected by intervention.
- **No formal RAF demonstration:** food-generation and catalytic closure have not been verified using a specified catalytic reaction system.
- **No autonomous life claim:** there is no demonstrated self-sustained compartment, long-term organizational establishment, heritable reproduction, or open-ended evolution.
- **Incomplete publication-level raw-data packaging:** detailed E013/E014 result JSON and the exact historical batch settings currently reside in the local research environment; this draft records the user's terminal summaries and the reproducible analysis scripts. Full replication requires publishing verified sanitized data/settings or an independently reproducible launch configuration.

### 8. Next experiments

**Priority A — step-543 fate audit.** Trace particle-level continuity, lineage reassignment, fragmentation, local nutrient changes and catalytic activity around steps 535–550. Distinguish detection loss from actual dissolution.

**Priority B — prospective replication.** Predefine candidate discovery thresholds, intervention selection, control matching, and outcomes before running fresh independent seeds. Report the full denominator, including negative experiments.

**Priority C — formal RAF mapping.** Specify reaction transformations, catalysts, food molecules/resources, spatial constraints, and tests of food generation/reflexive autocatalytic support. Distinguish *potential* closure from realized reactions and experimentally supported self-maintenance.

**Priority D — sustained spatial organization.** Explore endogenous concentration/retention mechanisms while avoiding explicitly programmed organisms, reproduction, fitness or maintenance objectives.

### 9. Conclusion

In one reconstructed computational trajectory, selective inhibition of catalysis associated with a transiently organized lineage changed its functional state while an active, though incompletely matched, off-target inhibition did not. The tracked lineage nonetheless disappeared at step 543 across all conditions.

This is preliminary evidence that *functional organizational maintenance* and *tracked structural persistence* can be experimentally dissociated in the chosen computational model. Generality, formal RAF closure and genuinely self-maintaining life-like organization remain unresolved.
